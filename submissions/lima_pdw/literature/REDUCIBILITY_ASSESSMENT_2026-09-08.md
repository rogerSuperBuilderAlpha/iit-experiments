## Computer-Mediated Communication Competence (lines 91–99)

**Citations:** Spitzberg (2006) — `spitzberg2006`, **full_text**, JCMC open access, read cover to
cover. Spitzberg & Cupach (1984) is not cited in this hearing (it appears later, in the construct
section, out of scope here). Clark & Brennan (1991) — **no card exists** (`clarkbrennan1991`
missing). Grounding theory ("Grounding in Communication," in *Perspectives on Socially Shared
Cognition*) is foundational in CSCW/psycholinguistics — one of the most-cited papers in the CMC
canon — so the canonicity risk is low, but the project has never independently verified the
"acknowledgment / next turn / repair" tripartite reading at line 97 against the actual chapter. This
is the paragraph's whole argument, not a footnote.

**Read depth:** strong for the anchor source, a real gap for the second.

**Field canonicity (assessor's judgment):** Spitzberg's competence model is a standard reference in
communication-competence scholarship; an OS/OT reader will not necessarily know it by name, but the
five-criteria list (appropriateness, effectiveness, coorientation, satisfaction, relationship
development) is exactly the kind of enumerated framework a reader can absorb from one sentence.

**Recommendation:** compress cautiously. Line 93's setup paragraph (the medium-as-parameter premise)
and line 95's full five-criteria list are restating a framework whose citation already carries it —
safe to cut to naming appropriateness and effectiveness only, since those are the two the paper
actually inherits (line 99 already says so). Line 97, the grounding-theory move, is the hearing's
one original argument and is not safe to compress until Clark & Brennan is verified — it is currently
built on an unread source.

**What a new paper would buy:** Clark & Brennan (1991), "Grounding in Communication," in *Perspectives
on Socially Shared Cognition* (Resnick, Levine & Teasley, eds., APA, 1991), pp. 127–149. A widely
available chapter; likely obtainable through most university libraries or as a commonly-circulated
PDF. Verifying it would make line 97 safe to cut harder, not just safe to keep.

**Paragraph 1 (line 139), the field-level claim.** Cites Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum (2025)

**Paragraph 2 (line 141), the folk-theories rebuttal.** Cites Bucher (2017) `bucher2017` —
