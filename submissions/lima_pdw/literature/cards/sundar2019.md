## What it argues

Sundar and Kim test the **machine heuristic**: the rule of thumb that machines are more objective, precise, and secure than humans, and therefore safer to disclose to. A scenario-based, between-subjects experiment (*N* = 160, MTurk, US residents) showed participants an identical airline-booking chat transcript ending in a request for a credit-card number, attributed either to a human sales representative or to Siri. H1 (machine cue increases disclosure intention) was supported: the machine-cue condition produced significantly higher disclosure intention (least-square mean 4.67 vs. 3.63 on a 7-point scale), *F*(1,121) = 8.72, *p* < .001, partial η² = .07. Belief in the machine heuristic (a validated 5-item scale, α = .87) had a significant main effect on disclosure, *F*(1,121) = 17.1, *p* < .0001, partial η² = .12, and — the key finding — moderated the machine-cue effect: *F*(1,121) = 6.34, *p* < .05, partial η² = .05 (H2), so the machine-cue advantage concentrated among participants who already endorsed the heuristic more strongly. All results held controlling for dispositional trust, general privacy concern, and power-usership. The manipulation check confirmed participants correctly perceived the source (*t*(158) = 44.85, *p* < .001, Cohen's *d* = 7.10). The finding inverts the usual automation-distrust story: for some tasks, machine attribution is a trust *advantage*, and the effect is conditional on a measurable individual difference, not universal.

## Relation to the argument

Directly relevant to §10's stratified fluency and to §7's asymmetric interpretation, because it names an individual difference in how a worker construes the system. A worker who endorses the machine heuristic reads a gate's determination as objective and therefore as information about her work; a worker who does not reads it as an artefact of a rule with interests behind it. **Those two workers will do completely different interpretive work on the same outcome, and only the second is doing what §7 calls asymmetric interpretation.**

That is a candidate mechanism for variance — and a rival to the competency account, since heuristic endorsement is a belief rather than a capacity and could be measured directly. §11 should include it, either as a control or as a component: if algorithmacy scores collapse once machine-heuristic endorsement is partialled out, the construct is measuring a disposition, not a competency.

## Caution

A short CHI paper, MTurk sample (86 male/74 female, mean age 38.29, 71.9% Caucasian), single scenario (a self-reported *intention* to disclose, not observed behavior), single context (credit-card disclosure to a chat agent). The machine heuristic is Sundar's construct from the MAIN model and its measurement is a short self-report scale; the paper's own limitations section flags that an "authority heuristic" (Siri as an Apple product) was tested as a rival explanation and ruled out (both proposed authority-heuristic mediators were non-significant, *p* = .90 and *p* = .79). Do not extend the trust finding to evaluative gates without saying it is an extrapolation. Kim is second author.
