## 2. Part A: connective tissue to protect

Each entry gives the location, the passage (quoted or located), and what a reader loses if a cutter takes it. I have grouped them by the job they do, because a length pass tends to target a job ("cut the signposts," "cut the forward references") rather than a sentence.

### A1. Sentences that tell the reader why the next block exists

These are the ones a cutter finds easiest to remove, because each is short, contains no citation, and states no finding. Every one of them is the answer to "why am I reading this?"

- **35, last sentence.** "Eight candidate constructs already circulate in adjacent literatures, and I give each a hearing before coining a new one." The only sentence that tells an organization-theory reader why a construct paper is about to spend 110 lines in communication studies and HCI. Without it the section heading "Extant Constructs" arrives as a literature review rather than as a due-process argument for coining.
- **43, "I take the eight in the order of standing they grant the machine, which puts human–machine communication first and gives it the longest hearing: no other program has moved the machine as far toward the position of a party."** The ordering principle. A reader who does not have it experiences the four HMC subsections as disproportion; with it, the length is a decision she can see.
- **83, "Roboticists have built and tested the machine-in-the-middle position, which the demarcation has to accommodate rather than deny. What the systematic review does not contain is the other half."** The pivot that makes the robotics evidence part of the argument (see verdict for the relocation).
- **89, last three sentences.** "Nothing in the field's equipment rules that position out; what is missing is the account of what the person on the far side must be able to do. Human–machine communication defined itself against the conduit tradition it inherited, and that tradition still holds the field's most developed account of what competence in a mediated exchange consists of. It is the next hearing." The bridge from HMC to Spitzberg. This is the one place where the paper explains why, having ordered the eight by the standing they grant the machine, it now steps *down* that order to a conduit model. Cut it and the Spitzberg section reads as a non sequitur.
- **99, last two sentences.** "Spitzberg models what competence *is* in a mediated dyad. The strongest account of how communicators *adapt* to what a medium withholds belongs to a different lineage." The is/adapt contrast is the reader's only handle on why two CMC hearings follow one another.
- **107, last two sentences.** "Experience with a channel is one account of where the capacity comes from. Formal instruction in the technology is another." The bridge into AI literacy, and the sentence that turns the next hearing into a test of the acquisition property rather than a coverage exercise.
- **113, "and I perform that derivation in the next section rather than asserting it."** The promise 165 keeps. The prior panel counted the performed derivation among the paper's strengths; a reader only recognizes it as performed because this clause set it up.
- **125, last sentence.** "Those dynamics discipline the framework developed here, because they show individual adaptation often deepening subordination." The forward link from Rahman to the Paradox at 193. Without it, 193's "individual mastery of the arrangement stabilizes the arrangement" arrives as a fresh idea instead of one the reader was told to expect sixty lines earlier.
- **147, last sentence.** "What organizational scholarship needs is a communicative construct built for the triadic position the empirical work has uncovered and left unequipped." The hook out of the comparison into the construct section.
- **195.** "Three propositions follow from this tension, and each clarifies why algorithmacy is not an unalloyed individual or organizational benefit." Nineteen words that stop a reader taking the propositions as advocacy.
- **215, first sentence.** "A critic might read the site as an ordinary hybrid of pedagogical hierarchy, peer review, and automated grading, and three properties of the gate refuse that reading." The sentence that tells the reader the next hundred words are an argument that the classroom is an instance of the form, not a description of a course.

### A2. Sentences that convert a foreign literature into the paper's own terms

The paper's eight hearings work for a non-specialist because each ends by saying what the finding means *here*. A cutter who trims "the last sentence of each hearing" as summary will remove the only sentence in the hearing the organization-theory reader can hold.

- **65, "That result is the experimental ground for the first operation this paper defines. An actor reading an unexplained outcome at a gate is deciding whether what arrived came from a peer, from a rule, or from the two together, and *interpreting* names the capacity to do it under conditions where no cover story fixes the answer."** Without these two sentences the Sundar and Nass paragraph is a communication-studies digression about cover stories.
- **67, first sentence.** "What the tradition establishes, it establishes for two positions: the machine is a social actor *toward* the participant and never a party stationed between the participant and someone else." The verdict on thirty years of work in one sentence; the paragraph's other content (mindlessness, the machine heuristic) hangs from it.
- **75, "A purely structural node count therefore fails to differentiate the framework from algorithmacy, because Hancock and colleagues already model two humans and a computational agent."** This is what makes 79's "categorical rather than parametric" necessary. A reader who does not see that AI-MC already has three nodes cannot see why the paper needs a second axis to separate itself from it.
- **77, last sentence.** "In none of these studies does the algorithm issue a determination binding both parties before either acts; what the record supplies is the mechanism by which a far-side person pays for a system's involvement in work she did not watch being done." The sentence that tells the reader what the three experiments (Jakesch, Hohenstein and Jung, Hohenstein et al.) were *for*.
- **83, "Traeger's result runs forward into the propositions as well … which is why Proposition 3 below treats transparency as an institutional lever rather than a fixed perimeter."** The only empirical grounding Proposition 3 receives anywhere in the body. - **97, "Grounding theory states why the mutual calibration Spitzberg's criteria require is unavailable rather than merely difficult."** The sentence that explains why a second theory (Clark and Brennan) enters mid-hearing. It sits inside the item-1 repair zone and will be reworded; keep its function.
- **103, last sentence.** "A reviewer who knows this literature will ask how algorithmacy is anything but social information processing with an algorithm swapped in for the human receiver." The question 105 answers. The Walther hearing is the paper's best for an outsider because this sentence tells the outsider what the stakes are before the answer comes.
- **105, whole paragraph**, and in particular "Walther's communicator adapts to a channel. The triad's participant answers to one." In the item-1 zone; the synthesis says it survives the correction untouched. Nothing here is cuttable.
- **129, "If those two explain the variance in coordination outcomes, algorithmacy explains nothing a labor-process account has not already explained better, and no amount of construct clarity settles the question.
