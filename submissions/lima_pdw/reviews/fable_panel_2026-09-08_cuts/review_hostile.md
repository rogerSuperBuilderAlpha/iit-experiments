# Hostile-reviewer lens, run in reverse: the do-not-cut floor and the safe cuts

Target: `manuscript/PAPER.md` as of 2026-09-08 — body 10,219 words (Introduction through Conclusion, lines 13–227), abstract 214, References 99 entries, Appendices A–C. Line numbers below are line numbers in that file. The 2 September panel's item 1 (the counterpart is "unseen" in the construct and visible at the site) is treated as known and unfixed; every recommendation below is checked against whether it makes that repair harder.

I read as the referee who knows Walther, Hancock, Zhou and Rahman, who cites Suddaby (2010) and Whetten (1989) back at the author, and who is looking for the sentence that lets him write "the construct is not differentiated from X" or "the author claims more than a two-transcript pilot can bear." The question for each passage is whether that referee gets his opening if the passage goes.

## 1. Verdict: how much slack sits between the current length and the floor

About 550 body words are hedging, self-narration of the paper's own care, signposting, or verbatim repetition, and cutting them makes the paper harder to attack, not easier. Roughly another 70 words are the author's call (the PDW-only Dissertation Coherence subsection). That puts the evidentiary floor near **9,650 body words**. Below that, cuts start landing on the eight hearings, the derivation, the scope paragraph, and the site paragraph — and each of those is where a hostile referee's first three objections are already answered.

Two things follow that the author should hear before cutting further.

First, the 2 September panel priced its restorations at 550–650 words. The safe cuts below recover almost exactly that. The honest net is a body that stays near 10,200 and reads tighter, not one that drops to 9,000. A body at 9,000 words is reachable only by removing differentiation, and a construct paper's differentiation is its contribution. The constructs hearings are not literature review; they are the argument.

Second, the appendices carry about 275 words of over-justification that a hostile reader treats as exposure rather than support (the Trinidad strategic-site paragraphs argue for a site that yields no Paper 3 data). Those are the cheapest cuts in the document and they cost nothing in the body count the author is watching. If the target is total length rather than body length, start there.

What the paper does well from this lens, and what the floor protects: it names its own kill-shot (Rahman's dependence and shocks, line 129) and routes it to a design; it turns Elish's crumple zone on itself (193); it tells a one-transcript pilot as a disconfirming outcome rather than support (173, 221, 524); it states the width of "derived" (171) and the width of "novel" (141). Those are the moves referees ask for and rarely get. Nothing in Part B touches them.

## 3. Part B: safe cuts, ranked

Ranked by words recovered weighted by confidence. Tier 1 is self-narration, signposting and verbatim repetition; nothing in it is argued anywhere else in the paper and nothing in it answers an objection. Tier 2 cuts require one replacement clause. Tier 3 is the author's call. Word counts are exact from the file; "net" subtracts the replacement where one is needed.

The test applied to each: does a hostile referee, reading the paper with the passage gone, gain a sentence he could not write before? For every entry below the answer is no, and for most the referee loses one — a sentence commenting on the paper's own care is a sentence he can quote as anxiety.

### Tier 1 — cut outright

**B21. Line 221 — two duplicates of Appendix B. Net 64.**
(a) "Each participant generates a self-derived identifier at intake and re-enters it across waves, so the panel is researcher-blind by procedure; with a cohort of roughly fifty I cannot rule out incidental recognition, and the coding protocol strips identifying particulars for that reason." (42) — repeated almost verbatim at B515. Replace with "The panel is researcher-blind by procedure (Appendix B)." (8). Net 34.
(b) "The first pilot administration produced the outcome that cuts against the construct — machine-only routing of the explanation, with the peer never addressed — and it obligates the protocol to treat that pattern as a live rival outcome rather than a variant of dual-audience encoding. I added probes that distinguish the two before the second administration." (56) — this is the third telling of the same transcript (173, 221, B524), and by the third a referee starts to notice how much weight one N = 1 result is carrying. Replace with "The first pilot administration produced the disconfirming pattern for *specifying intent* — machine-only routing, peer never addressed — and I added probes before the second administration." (26). Net 30.
*Why no exposure:* the construct-level use (173) and the protocol-level use (B524) both survive; the illustration item 1 needs ("the same act is read by both parties") stays at 173. "The status, plainly:" in the same paragraph — cut "plainly"; the paragraph is plain without announcing it (1).
