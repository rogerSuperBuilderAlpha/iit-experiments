## 1. Overall verdict: the review is doing the derivation's share of the paper

The order of sections matches the convention exactly. Gap, candidate constructs, definition and
scope, semantic relations, derivation of constitutive elements, boundary of the term, acquisition,
implications and propositions, empirical strategy, conclusion: nothing is out of sequence at the
section level, and no section a construct paper needs is missing. The problem is proportion, not
sequence.

| Section | Words | Share of body | Construct-paper norm (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 819 | 8% | 8–12% |
| Coordination-Form Gap | 340 | 3% | 5–10% (often folded into the Introduction) |
| Extant Constructs (all) | 5,634 | **56%** | 25–35% |
| — of which Human–Machine Communication + AI-Mediated Communication (L59–L89) | 1,837 | 18% | one candidate's share would be 3–5% |
| — of which "What These Boundaries Share" (L137–L147) | 611 | 6% | 3–4% |
| Construct section (L149–L207) | 2,366 | **23%** | 35–45% |
| Empirical Strategy | 691 | 7% | 5–10% for a theory paper with a pilot |
| Conclusion | 239 | 2% | 5–8% |

The review runs at two and a half times the length of the construct section. Whetten's test for a
contribution is what the paper adds, and Suddaby's is whether the definition, scope, semantic
relations and coherence are on the page; both locate the paper's value in the construct section,
and a reader who weighs by page count will conclude that this is a literature review with a
construct appended. It is not — the derivation at L165 is real and the synthesis called it the
paper's strongest passage — but the proportions say otherwise.

Three structural facts explain the excess, and each yields cuts that cost nothing:

**(a) The review states its criteria after applying them.** L39 poses the diagnostic question
(conduit, interlocutor, or third party). L43 poses it again in different words (where is the
counterpart?) and promises that "the four analytical conditions stated at the close of this section
formalize the criteria I apply across all of them." L145, six thousand words later, states the four
conditions and closes with "I applied them in every hearing above." A construct paper states the
criteria once, before the table, and then applies them. Merging L39, L43 and L145 into one criteria
paragraph ahead of Table 1 recovers about 120 words and fixes the inversion.

**(b) One candidate gets four sub-hearings and a recap.** Human–machine communication is heard as
a sequence (social actor → communicative subject → delegate → machine among groups → Suchman →
demarcation), and the trajectory is narrated twice: L61 previews it and L89 restates it, down to a
repeated phrase ("nearer to more humans and nearer to more consequence" appears in both). The
justification given at L43 — no other program has moved the machine as far toward the position of a
party — earns the longest hearing; it does not earn the hearing twice.

**(c) The closing sections re-litigate settled material.** The Conclusion (L225) reopens the
measurement model, the molar/molecular distinction from L181, and the four semantic relations from
L159. Theoretical Implications (L189) repeats the online-dispute-resolution and Curchod/Cameron
material from L143, and its "micro-foundational engine macro-structural models presuppose" sentence
recurs verbatim at L227. A construct paper's conclusion restates the one-sentence claim, the
contribution and what remains open; it does not re-argue.

Two further observations cost no words but bear on the convention. The construct is defined in the
Introduction (L21) and the section headed "A Construct for Coordinative Co-optation" never restates
the definition; Suddaby's first criterion is a definition in the construct section, and a reader
turning to L149 for it will not find one. And "Semantic Relations" (L157) precedes the derivation
(L161) while referring to *interpreting* and *specifying intent* by name — those terms are not
derived until L165. Moving L157–L159 to follow L175 costs nothing and reads as Suddaby's order
(define, derive, then relate). Neither is a cut; both are the kind of change the author can make
while cutting.

The Introduction, the Gap section, the propositions and the Empirical Strategy are at or below
convention and should not lose much. The Conclusion is under length and should be rewritten rather
than cut; the words recovered from L225's re-litigation can go to what the non-specialist asked
for last round (return to the student, the inequality and Lima).
