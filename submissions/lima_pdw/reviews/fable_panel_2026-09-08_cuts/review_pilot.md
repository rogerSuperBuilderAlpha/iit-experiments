## 1. Overall verdict

The body's empirical material is already sized for a pilot. The Empirical Strategy section runs 703
words, seven percent of the body, and its four paragraphs each do one job: 213 separates the action
research from the study, 215 argues the site's correspondence to the form, 219 states the research
questions, 221 gives the status with the honest N. A construct-development paper that reports two
transcripts cannot say much less than that and still tell a reader where the construct was observed.
I recommend 70 words of body cuts in total, both of them removals of restated material, and nothing
from 213, 215 or 219.

Appendix B is where the pilot runs long. It is 1,981 words — nearly three times the body's empirical
section — for a study whose corpus is one transcript read informally and one unread. Roughly 45
percent of it is one of three things: a restatement of what the body already says (the site at 488,
the researcher-blind procedure at 515, the core/thesis separation at 484, the research questions at
454–456, the pilot outcome at 524), a full-study defense of decisions the pilot does not yet need
defended (the Merton/Eisenhardt strategic-site warrant at 472–474, the four-phase action research
definition at 460, the Pratt "rigor by alignment" sentence at 456), or a claim the two transcripts
cannot support (elicitation "under three different concealment regimes" at 482, "demographic shifts
isolated behavioral variance" at 468). The third category is the one to notice: cutting it makes
the paper more honest about the pilot, not less.

The pilot's status is stated four times — body 221, Conclusion 225, Appendix B 484, Appendix B 521
— and I would keep all four, because each carries something the others do not (the N, the
contribution claim, the developmental-versus-formal distinction, the read/unread disclosure). The
"this is only a pilot" restatements are not the padding. The protocol justification and the site
history are.

Two interactions with the 2026-09-02 synthesis matter for the total. First, the synthesis asks
Appendix B to *grow* by one disclosure paragraph on who retunes the gate and on what schedule (item
5), by consent, transcript custody and interviewer-prompt-version disclosures (methodology F10 — the
word "consent" appears in the paper only inside Cameron's title), and by roughly 60 words of
abductive logic moved into the body (item 12, line 221). My Appendix B cuts of about 760 words leave
room for those additions with net savings of roughly 600. Second, the largest single cut (472–474)
orphans six references that appear nowhere else in the paper, which removes about 160 further words
from the reference list.

**A5. Appendix B, line 517, one sentence.** "I reserve large-sample discriminant testing for Study

### Tier B — full-study defenses the pilot does not need

**A7. Appendix B, lines 472–474, the two paragraphs under "The Trinidad Field Setting as a
Strategic Research Site."** — 174 words, net ~170. This is the largest over-elaboration in the
paper. It argues a Mertonian strategic-site warrant and an Eisenhardt-and-Graebner single-case
justification, backed by Bamberger and Pratt on unconventional contexts and Bothello et al. on
institutional voids, for cohorts that the same appendix says are "the developmental foundation
rather than the formal empirical corpus" (484). The 2026-09-02 methodology reviewer made the point
(F7): the warrant is argued for the Trinidad cohorts, which yield no Paper 3 data. A site-selection
defense of this weight belongs to the paper that reports data from the site; for a two-transcript
pilot it reads as defending a study that has not been run. The developmental role of the early
cohorts survives in 484's second sentence and in the cohort list at 462. Six references are cited
only here — Merton 1987, Eisenhardt & Graebner 2007, ILO 2025, Chigbu 2026, Bamberger & Pratt 2010,
Bothello et al. 2019 — and would leave the reference list (about 160 words). One consideration the
author may weigh against this: the Caribbean origin has rhetorical value at a Lima workshop. If so,
a single clause is enough, and 462 ("launched in Trinidad and Tobago") already supplies it.

**A8. Table B1, the "Piloting Milestone" column (lines 531–536).** — about 55 words across the
header and four cells, net 55. The column restates the pilot findings list at 521–525 a second time

## 4. What I would not cut, and why

**Body line 173, the pilot sentence in the derivation.** The one place the pilot does theoretical
rather than reporting work: it supplies the disconfirming pattern for *specifying intent* as an
observed outcome, which is what a pilot in a construct paper is for. It is in the contradiction zone
(173 is on the list) and should stay untouched until item 1 is fixed. Cutting 221's and 524's
restatements (A13, A14) is what makes this instance the single telling.

**Body line 215, the site paragraph.** The last panel called it disclosure rather than assertion,
and it carries the mutual-bindingness argument, the unchosen-reviewer matching, the retuning, the
distinction between codebase drift (publicly legible, excluded from *keeping track*) and rule drift,
and the undocumented escalation-rights caveat. Two hundred fifty-two words for the paper's only
argument that the site instantiates the form is not long. Its first clause ("an aggregated score
she cannot inspect") is also the correct framing — opacity of consequence — that the item 1 repair
will lean on.

**Body line 213, the no-further-interventions pledge.** Synthesis item 5 shows this pledge in
tension with the retuning at 215. The tension is for the author to resolve by disclosure, and the
pledge must stay visible so that the resolution can be checked against it.

**Body line 219, the four research questions.** Item 6 asks for about twenty words added here.
Nothing to cut.

**Body line 221, the status sentence.** IRB determination and protocol number, cohort size and
timing, "two pilot transcripts exist, one read informally and one unread," "none of the formal
corpus has been collected or coded," and the researcher-blind procedure with its incidental-
recognition caveat. This is the disclosure a PDW reader is owed and the last panel praised it. I cut
only the finding restated after it.

**Appendix B line 479, the visibility fact.** Named in synthesis item 1. It must survive any
reorganization of the three-regime list (A16), relocated rather than removed.

**Appendix B line 490, first two sentences.** "Because student participants construct and inspect
the codebase itself, the opacity does not come from hidden source code; it comes from concealed
aggregation weights and shifting decision thresholds governing peer evaluation." This states what is
actually concealed at the site — weights, not code and not reviews — and is the sentence the item 1
repair should be built on. Keep.

**Appendix B line 492, positionality and safeguards.** Anonymization at intake before any record
reaches the researcher, no instructor vote or override, interviews only after evaluative cycles
close, the audit trail. This is the closest thing the paper has to an ethics disclosure, and the
methodology reviewer (F10) found it insufficient: no consent statement, no transcript custody, no
opt-in-versus-required, no interviewer-prompt version. The paragraph should grow, not shrink. The
insider-research citations (Riordan via Coghlan & Brannick, Anteby, Ferguson et al., Mercer, Pratt
et al. 2020, Tracy) are cheap and appropriate for a student-participant site.

**Appendix B line 503, the protocol as administered.** The critical incident structure (Flanagan),
the incident-forcing and reframing behavior, the omission of theoretical vocabulary, and the
client-side redaction before the participant reviews and validates the transcript. This is what the
pilot *is*; the redaction sentence doubles as the paper's only account of how anonymity is produced.
Every word stays.

**Appendix B lines 505 (lead sentence) and 511, the abductive logic and the pre-specified negative
case.** The last panel asked for these to appear in the body as well (methodology F9, item 12).
Line 511's negative case — a participant who resolves breakdowns through technical mastery alone,
never conceptualizing the peer — is the falsification criterion for the construct's necessity.
Nothing in the pilot's evidentiary standing survives without it.

**Appendix B lines 521–523, the pilot N and the elicitation finding.** "Two pilot administrations
of the in-editor protocol exist. I read the first informally to check that the modules function; the
second transcript is unread" is the sentence a hostile reader will look for. Item 1 of the list
(523) is the evidence the instrument produces a usable incident at all. Keep both.

**Appendix B line 517, first two sentences and the IRB line.** What the panel is for (within-person
trajectories; Zhou's scale at wave 2 as a convergence check; the machine-heuristic measure), and
that it is not powered for factor analysis. The IRB determination is stated here and at 221; the
duplication costs thirteen words and buys nothing but insurance, and I would leave it.

**Table B1, the Target Data and Falsification columns.** The falsification column is the apparatus
the last panel praised and the methodology reviewer wants extended (a row for the recursive-process
claim; a gate-weight threshold). Row 534's target-data cell contains "aimed at unseen peer
evaluators" — in the zone by vocabulary; leave it for the item 1 pass.

**Appendix C lines 546 and 554.** The abductive sufficiency criterion with its alternative-casing
clause, and the pooling condition. Both are falsification commitments; 554's "freezing the gate
configuration" is due for rewording under item 5, not removal.

**The four "this is a pilot" statements (221, 225, 484, 521).** Each carries a distinct fact — the N,
the contribution claim, the developmental-versus-formal distinction, the read/unread status. They do
not restate one another and a reader who lands on any one section gets the caveat it needs.

**Appendix A and the Dissertation Coherence note.** Outside this lens. Appendix A is Paper 1's
summary and not empirical material; the coherence note is already marked PDW-only.

One thing I considered and rejected: cutting the five-cohort list at 462–466 to a single sentence.
It is 125 words of programme history for cohorts that yield no data, and it tempted me. I kept it
because it is the only place the paper says where the instrument was developed and over what span,
which is disclosure a reader of a two-transcript pilot is entitled to, and because 484's second
sentence needs the list to refer back to. Trimming 463 (A15) is the proportionate version.
