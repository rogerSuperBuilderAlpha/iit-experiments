## Verdict

**Minor revisions, leaning moderate.** The compression held. Each of the eight hearings still reads its source at the level of definitions, items, and page-anchored quotations, each still dismisses its candidate for a different named reason, and the demarcation survives in every one. The Walther hearing (lines 103–107) and the AI-mediated communication block (75–79) are as sharp at half length as they were at full. No hearing now misrepresents the central claim of its primary source.

What the cut cost sits at the margins, which is where a communication-studies discussant reads. Four compressed sentences now say more than the cards license: Litt (2012) is bundled into a description of algorithmic triads she never wrote about (19, 141); Abidin (2016) is folded under a three-part rebuttal that fits Cotter and not her (141); "only two of the seventeen reference human actors" (111) is a count a reviewer with Long and Magerko's table can contest; and the Zhou et al. antecedents sentence (121) calls peer support "primary" and adds a collectivist-orientation moderator neither Zhou card records. None misattributes a thesis. All four are the kind of overreach a hostile specialist collects.

The one content problem that is not marginal predates the cut and now stands more exposed: the grounding argument (97, 171) says the counterpart returns "no acknowledgment the actor can read," while the paper's own opening scene (15) and site description (479) have peer comments and votes publicly inspectable. The argument needs restating as a severance of evidence from consequence — which is what the Spitzberg paragraph two sentences earlier already says. Fixing that sentence also fixes the account of why *interpreting* and *specifying intent* split.

## Part 1 — Hearing-by-hearing accuracy and demarcation

### Human–Machine Communication (59–89; 3,330 words → ~1,850)

The architecture survives — four positions on one trajectory, Suchman's asymmetry beneath, the demarcation as the next position on the line — and it still lands: "the machine above both humans, reading each of them and returning a determination that binds them both" (89).

**Finding 1 (medium). The trajectory is presented as history and is an analytic ordering.** Line 61: "Over three decades the field has moved its object outward — from a desktop computer ... to a communicative subject ... to a delegate ... to a participant among several people at once." Line 75: "The field's next generation moved back toward two humans." The dates do not run that way. Jung, Martelaro and Hinds put a robot between two humans in 2015; Guzman's volume named the communicative-subject position in 2018; Guzman and Lewis, Hancock et al., and Traeger et al. all appeared in 2020. Robots-among-groups precedes the communicative subject, and AI-MC is its contemporary, not its successor. The paper states the right principle at 43 — "I take the eight in the order of standing they grant the machine" — and 61 and 75 contradict it. An HMC referee will read "next generation" as a claim about their field's history. **Fix:** at 61, "ordered by the standing each grants the machine rather than by date"; at 75, "The delegate position moves back toward two humans." The ladder of standing was always the argument.

**Finding 2 (low). Two evidentiary sentences left with the survey and the assertions they carried stayed.** Line 67 asserts "What the tradition establishes, it establishes for two positions" without the pre-cut sentence that established it — "across two hundred studies the design does not vary in that respect (Reeves & Nass, 1996)," which the card calls stronger than any programmatic statement because it is a demonstrated design. Line 69's handbook sentence lost its concession that multi-party settings appear in the CSCW and teaming chapters; the card warns against reading a table of contents as proof that no chapter addresses triads. The sentence is still true and now invites the objection it used to pre-empt. **Fix:** restore both, ~45 words (Part 3).

**Finding 3 (low–medium). Fortunati and Edwards (2020) left, and with them the field's own definition.** The demarcation at 89 now rests on an agenda article and a handbook's organization. The founding editorial stated the constitutive unit outright — meaning-making *between humans and machines* — "the dyad, stated as a definition rather than inferred from practice" (card). The cut plan removed it as a duplicate of the Communicative Subject block; it duplicated the claim, not the evidence. **Fix:** one sentence at 89, ~35 words.

Confirmed: the Sundar and Nass four-source design and "user supplies it" reading match the card, with the qualification the card insists on kept — their manipulation was a stated cover story, the gate's is not (65). One precision note at 77: Hohenstein and Jung's trust-in-partner gain was a main effect and the blame shift the conditional finding; the sentence fuses them. Predates the cut.

### AI-Mediated Communication (75–79)

Accurate throughout: the p. 90 body definition, the five parameters, the optimization-goal axis, "An editor is not a judge." The one unconfirmed anchor is "(p. 89)" on "the message receiver is assumed to understand and accept that agency" — the steelman has the quotation verbatim and no page.

**Finding 4 (low). The strongest form of the demarcation is in Hancock's own vocabulary and neither version used it.** The steelman's test: before a system delegated by neither party, "every one of their parameters loses its referent" — role orientation has no role, optimization goal has no principal, autonomy has no sender to supervise. Line 79 gestures at it; one sentence makes it a result about their instrument rather than a metaphor about their agent.

**Finding 5 (low–medium). Mieczkowski et al. (2021) left the hearing and took the grounding foil with it.** The pre-cut described their referential communication task as "a study of *grounding* ... in which both parties are present and in contact throughout" — the empirical bridge between the AI-MC record and the Clark and Brennan argument at 97: the one study to measure grounding through an algorithm did so with both parties co-present, the condition the triad removes. Mieczkowski survives correctly in Appendix C (552), so nothing is orphaned; what is lost is the contrast that made "grounding cannot close" empirical. **Fix:** ~40 words at 77 or 97.

### Computer-Mediated Communication Competence (93–99)

The hearing opens on the wording the prior round recommended, and the triadic-referents paragraph (99) resolves last round's Finding 1 cleanly.

**Finding 6 (medium–high). The grounding claim is too strong for the paper's own site, and it sits beside the correct version.** Line 97: "The intermediary severs the link between the counterpart's evaluation and its consequence: her appraisal reaches the focal actor only as an isolated input to an opaque aggregation neither party controls." Right. Four sentences on: "Every one of those forms of evidence is structurally withheld in the triad. The counterpart returns no acknowledgment the actor can read, no next turn addressed to her, and no route to repair." At the flagship site she returns all three. Line 15: classmates "read it, comment on it, and vote, all in public where she can watch." Line 479: "qualitative peer reviews and upvotes remained publicly inspectable while the underlying aggregate weighting remained concealed." A comment is acknowledgment; a thread is a next turn and a route to repair. What the site withholds is the *weight* that evidence carries in the verdict — uptake decoupled from consequence, as the earlier sentence says. Clark and Brennan supply the correct claim: grounding with the peer can partially close; grounding with the party whose verdict binds cannot, because a rule returns no evidence of understanding, and the verdict "is a judgment about understanding rather than evidence of it" (the paper's phrase, which survives). Dennis et al.'s *convergence* (163) says the same from the information-systems side. **Why it matters:** this paragraph is load-bearing twice — for the Spitzberg demarcation and, at 171, for the split between *interpreting* and *specifying intent*. A discussant who has read the opening scene will notice the student can watch her reviewers' comments land. **Fix:** rewrite the two sentences so the withheld thing is the evidence-to-consequence link; derive the split at 171 from the evaluator's silence (Part 2).

**Finding 7 (low). A page anchor floated.** Line 93 cites "(p. 638)" for "motivation, knowledge, skills, context, and relational outcomes"; pre-cut, p. 638 anchored the four skill clusters, and the card has motivation at 637. Drop or cite the range.

### AI Literacy (111–113)

**Finding 8 (medium). "Only two of the seventeen reference human actors" is the count the steelman warned against.** The steelman lists all seventeen and replaces the absolute form with a checkable one: "no competency names another person as a party to the interaction; the one competency that names humans at all names them as the system's designers." Competency 2 discusses "differences between human, animal, and machine intelligence"; Competency 12 names "one's own data"; Competency 16 names employment and privacy. A referee with the table will count more than two, in a hearing whose force is that it read the list. The Competency 10, Competency 5 and Design Consideration 11 readings are verbatim-correct. **Fix:** the steelman's positional form.

**Finding 9 (low–medium). The hearing understates Long and Magerko on perception.** Line 111 opens: "AI literacy frameworks treat the algorithm as an object of formal technical comprehension." Their fifth question — *How do people perceive AI?* — opens on folk theories that "whether accurate or not, shape the nature of user interaction," and their "Interpreting AI Systems" passage argues theory of mind misleads when the target is a machine. That is a hearing of interpretation under opacity inside the framework filed as technical comprehension. The derivation contrast at 113 survives (one competency under that theme, and it concerns programmability); fairness costs one clause.

One smaller note: "its artificial-intelligence material in an appendix" (111) is inexact — the card records 35 of 73 AI examples distributed through every competence area; the pre-cut "areas that never meet" was accurate.

### Algorithmic Competency (117–121)

**Finding 10 (medium). The antecedents sentence overstates the source and adds a moderator the cards do not contain.** Line 121: "the primary antecedents of algorithmic competency in their structural model are social — informal peer support moderated by collectivist orientation." ### What These Boundaries Share (139–147)

The coupling synthesis (139) is the paper's best paragraph on the comparison, and the four conditions with "No candidate meets all four" survive (145). Three findings in the rival paragraph.

**Finding 11 (low–medium). Litt (2012) is credited with an algorithm her article does not have.** Line 141: Bucher, DeVito and Litt "together describe a person managing self-presentation to human others through an opaque, adaptive, curating algorithm." Line 19: "In each case an opaque, adaptive system stands between a person and human others." The Litt card: the imagined audience is "calibrated against social knowledge of who is likely to be reading, not against any system that itself reads, interprets or judges the post," and "there is no third party in Litt's account." Litt belongs — she names the anticipatory move — but not inside the "through an algorithm" clause. **Fix:** "Bucher and DeVito describe ... through an opaque, adaptive, curating algorithm; Litt supplies the anticipatory construct with no algorithm in the picture." Adjust 19 to match.

**Finding 12 (medium). Abidin (2016) is filed under a rebuttal that does not fit her.** The cut plan said "the same three dimensions dispose of both" Cotter and Abidin. They do not. The dimensions at 141: the algorithm binds the presenter alone; the audience is an aggregate; no joint work. The Abidin card: her follower addresses "a *specific, named human being* ... not a diffuse audience," and Abidin excludes the algorithm by her own statement — "Unlike studies on algorithmic visibility (Bucher, 2012), visibility labour is concerned with analogue affective labour." Two of three dimensions are false of her; the pre-cut clause ("an identifiable human counterpart while bracketing computational algorithms") was right. The card also says why she earns her place: the one construct in the neighborhood with a genuine counterpart is the one that deliberately leaves out the algorithm — nobody has put both in at once. **Fix:** restore ~25 words or drop the cite.

**Finding 13 (medium). The DeVito pairing is prior art the paragraph does not concede.** The DeVito card: they "already link the two facets in a single mechanism: you theorise the opaque system *in order to* present yourself through it. That is asymmetric interpretation feeding intent specification, named, evidenced, and published seven years before this paper's derivation." The novelty claim is stated at the right width — mutual bindingness and joint work — and I do not re-raise it. But the *coupling* of interpreting to encoding, which the recursive-process paragraph (175) presents as a property of the construct, is DeVito's mechanism. The card's recommendation: concede the pairing to strengthen the derivation — "the pairing of inbound and outbound is established prior art, the derivation of why exactly these and no others is not." One sentence at 141, and it costs nothing the paper claims.

Two lesser notes. Line 139 calls Oeldorf-Hirsch and Neubaum (2025) and Iyamu et al. (2026) "systematic reviews"; the cards say narrative review and scoping review, and the abstract already dropped that label for the paper's own comparison. And the Manky (2025) mechanism (19, 147) — reading an opaque metric to decide whether to accept a passenger — is a reading of an abstract the card could not confirm against the article body, and flags as the paper's most load-bearing unverified empirical citation.

## Part 2 — Grounding of the three operations

**Source orientation → interpreting (65, 173).** Convincing, and the compression kept the qualification that matters. Sundar and Nass show identical content evaluated differently under machine, programmer, network and other-user attributions, with the user supplying the attribution; the paper's move — the participant does that work "under conditions where no cover story fixes the answer" — is the card's own recommended use ("the triad is not just a description of the arrangement; it is a problem the worker has to solve"). Line 173's "one signal from which to infer two minds" is the definition doing what the warrant licenses. Sundar and Kim's machine heuristic is correctly placed as a discriminant threat (159) and a Study 3 covariate (185), not a rival account of the arrangement.

**Grounding → the split (97, 171).** The logic is right and one sentence undercuts it. Line 171: "Without a grounding loop to join them, machine-facing decoding and counterpart-facing encoding become two unaided inferences rather than two halves of one process (Clark & Brennan, 1991), which is why the model splits *interpreting* from *specifying intent*." Two problems. First, Finding 6: the loop with the *counterpart* is not absent at the site; the loop with the *evaluator* is. The split should follow from the evaluator's silence — the party whose verdict binds returns no evidence of understanding, so decoding the verdict and encoding for the addressee cannot be calibrated against each other. Second, a compression artifact: the pre-cut read "inbound and outbound understanding"; the cut changed it to "machine-facing decoding and counterpart-facing encoding," which narrows *interpreting* to the machine when the definition at 167 has it reconstruct "both the algorithmic intermediary and the unseen human counterpart." The construct is dual-object on the inbound side; 171 now says otherwise. **Fix:** revert to "inbound and outbound"; make the reason the evaluator's silence. Daft and Lengel's equivocality (163) is correctly used and the card's caution — keep the uncertainty/equivocality distinction, treat the richness hierarchy as superseded — is respected. Dennis et al.'s *convergence* now arrives without its gloss ("reaching shared meaning about information both parties already hold" was cut); fifteen words restore it for the information-systems readers the venue will contain.

**Duran → keeping track (173).** Convincing and correctly bounded, exactly as the card reads it: adaptability is noticing that a situation changed and revising; the difference is what supplies the signal — "his communicator reads a partner and a setting she can see; the triad's actor reads outcomes from a rule that announces nothing." One inheritance never taken: Duran's 1992 self-critique found his scale drifted toward measuring social ease, a named hazard for *keeping track* items that Appendix C could carry in a clause.

**Bindingness → specifying intent (165).** The derivation the prior synthesis asked for is on the page — "Bindingness turns every message into evidence before a verdict, so the actor must encode for judge and counterpart at once" — and the pilot's machine-only routing is correctly narrated as the disconfirming pattern (173, 221). Closed.

**Spitzberg (2015) and the reversion (181).** Line 99 resolved last round's inheritance tension by relocating the evaluator: appropriateness read off the actor's reconstruction, effectiveness off gate-plus-decoding. Line 181 then adopts Spitzberg's ordering — competence is the *impression* that skills were appropriate and effective — "because a molar impression formed by an evaluator is exactly what the triad withholds." Under 99 the impression is relocated; under 181 it is withheld, and by Spitzberg's ordering an arrangement that withholds the impression leaves only skills. The two paragraphs should agree. Pre-cut had the same sentence. **Fix (low–medium):** at 181, "a molar impression formed by a co-present evaluator is what the triad withholds, and what the actor's reconstruction under *interpreting* must supply in its place." Separately, the Conclusion (225) attributes the molar/molecular no-free-inference rule to Spitzberg and Cupach (1984); the card places that rule in Spitzberg's later assessment work and recommends 1984 for the molar/molecular commitment, 2015 for the inference rule.

## Part 4 — What is strong

The eight hearings remain the paper's achievement and the compression did not blunt them. Each construct is read in its own vocabulary — Hancock's parameters, Spitzberg's outcome criteria, Zhou's items, Rahman's five properties, Sutherland's P39 — and each is dismissed for a different named reason; Table 1's "Structural Vacancy" column is still a map communication studies does not have of a fault line through its own subfields. The Walther hearing is the best writing in the paper. The AI-MC block ends its lineage on a mechanism — "a far-side person pays for a system's involvement in work she did not watch being done" — that does real work in the Paradox section. Suchman's "second interpreter behind" the asymmetry (87) is the paper's tightest statement of what the triad adds to human–machine interaction.

What the author inherits from each rejected framework is stated as plainly as what he rejects, and the inheritances have become design commitments: Rahman's dependence and shock history, Walther's cognitive slack, Nass and Moon's mindless scripting, and Sundar and Kim's machine heuristic all enter Study 3 as covariates (185). That is the difference between a paper that acknowledges rivals and one that arranges to be beaten by them. The novelty claim sits at the width the cards support, the four confirmed facts are stated flatly where they belong (215, 221), and turning Elish's crumple zone on the construct itself (193) is a move few competence papers make.
