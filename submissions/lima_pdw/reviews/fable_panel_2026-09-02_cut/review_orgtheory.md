## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor and argument structure

### Finding 1 — The definition and the derivation name different triples (severity: high; cheap to fix)

**Lines 21, 153, 155, 165, 215.** Line 21 gives the body's formal definition and then says: "Three terms in the definition carry the structure. Opacity means the participant cannot observe the governing rule directly; adaptivity means the rule itself moves over time; intermediary designates the algorithmic system standing between the focal actor and her human counterpart." Bindingness is not among them, and the definition sentence itself contains no verdict, no evaluation, no binding — while the abstract (line 9) defines the construct by an intermediary "that evaluates and binds them both." Line 153 then says "the operations derive from opacity, adaptivity, and bindingness," line 155 makes the construct apply where the intermediary is "opaque, adaptive, and mutually binding," and line 165 performs the derivation on exactly those three, with bindingness generating *specifying intent*.

Why it matters: the previous round's Finding 2 asked for the derivation to be shown, and it now is — cleanly. But "three properties, three deficits, three operations" holds only if the definition supplies the same three properties. As written, the definition's third term is a *position* (intermediary) and the derivation's third is a *property of that position* (bindingness), so *specifying intent* is derived from something the definition never names — and that something is the construct's differentiator in Table 1 (line 57), the four conditions (line 145), and the site paragraph (line 215).

Fix: rewrite the definition at line 21 to carry the property, not just the position — "…to interpret an opaque, adaptive intermediary *that evaluates and binds both parties*, and the counterpart reached through it…" — and change "Three terms" to name opacity, adaptivity, and bindingness, with "intermediary" glossed as the seat those three properties attach to. Then the abstract, the definition, the scope conditions, and the derivation all speak with one voice.

### Finding 2 — Manky's drivers are outside the triad at line 19 and inside it at lines 147 and 155 (severity: high)

**Lines 19, 147, 155.** The Introduction's narrowed-novelty paragraph lists four fragments — Bucher, DeVito, Litt, Manky — and closes them with one verdict: "in none of them does that system return a single determination binding both sides of a jointly executed task." True of a Facebook feed and an Instagram audience; not true of ride-hailing. A dispatch platform rates and can deactivate the passenger as well as the driver, and the match itself binds both parties to a ride neither chose the other for. The paper knows this: line 155 places "dispatch networks" at the *core* of the construct's domain, and line 147 reframes Manky as fieldwork that "already documents the latent capacity." So Manky's Lima drivers are outside the configuration at line 19 and inside its core scope at line 155, and the Introduction — in front of a Lima audience — gets the flagship naturalistic case wrong on the paper's own criterion.

Why it matters: the narrowed claim ("no *construct* models…") survives Manky comfortably, because Manky documents a practice, not a construct. But line 19 makes a claim about *settings*, and that version is false for one of its four members. A discussant who knows the Peruvian platform-work literature will catch it in two minutes.

Fix: separate Manky from the folk-theories trio at line 19. Something like: "Manky (2025) comes closest, and differs in kind: ride-hailing does bind driver and passenger alike, and his drivers exercise exactly the counterpart-directed reading this paper calls *interpreting*. What his study lacks is the construct, not the configuration." Then the paragraph's verdict applies to Bucher, DeVito, and Litt only, and the novelty claim is stated as what it is — a claim about constructs, which Manky supports rather than threatens.

### Finding 3 — The micro-foundations claim is earned as a category, not yet as a mechanism (severity: moderate)

**Lines 9, 35, 121, 153, 185, 189, 193, 227; Appendix C line 566.** The paper calls algorithmacy a micro-foundation five times and cites Felin, Foss, and Ployhart (2015) twice. Felin and colleagues ask three things of such a construct: a lower level than the phenomenon it explains, specified antecedents, and an *aggregation* mechanism from individual to collective — and they treat summation as the weakest such mechanism, because interaction and emergence are usually where the macro effect comes from. The paper satisfies the first two (lines 121, 153, 185). The third it asserts in one sentence at line 193: cohorts "differing in *mean* algorithmacy should therefore differ in attrition, contestation volume, and coordination failures."

Here the paper's own socialization property works against it. If algorithmacy is acquired "through shared practice and collective sensemaking" (line 121) and "through peer networks" (line 153), a cohort's mean algorithmacy and the cohort's shared working theory of the gate are one quantity measured twice, and the friction difference Study 3 predicts (line 566) could be an effect of the shared model rather than of individual capacity summed. That is the case where additive aggregation is the wrong function. The pre-cut text was no stronger here; this is a gap the previous panel did not raise.

Fix: one paragraph, at line 193 or in Study 3, stating that the aggregation function is not assumed additive; that cohort-level shared theory (DeVito et al.'s folk-theory consensus is a ready measure) enters Study 3 as a separate cohort-level variable; and that the construct's individual-level claim is tested *within* cohorts while the aggregation claim is tested *across* them. That converts an asserted micro-foundation into a designed one and is the strongest single addition available to the Theoretical Implications section.

### Finding 4 — RQ4's comparison partner drifted back to *keeping track* in the body (severity: moderate; re-opened by the cut)

**Lines 171, 219; Appendix B line 456; Appendix C line 548.** Lines 171, 456, and 548 now agree: attribution is provisionally housed in *interpreting*, and RQ4 tests whether it separates "from that operation and, secondarily, from longitudinal tracking" (456). The body's RQ4 sentence at line 219 does not agree. It asks whether attribution is "an independent fourth operation," then supplies only the tracking contrast ("An actor may detect that a standard has moved without diagnosing the locus of failure for a single outcome"). The pre-cut sentence naming both comparisons was removed, so a reader of the body alone reads RQ4 as a test against *keeping track* — the inconsistency the prior round fixed.

Fix: restore one clause at line 219 — "…and does resolving the locus of authority separate from *interpreting*, where the model provisionally houses it, and secondarily from *keeping track*?" — and drop the tracking-only gloss or keep it as the secondary comparison. Twenty words.

### Finding 5 — Proposition 3 has lost its footing in the scope conditions (severity: moderate; opened by the cut)

**Lines 155, 201.** Proposition 3 treats opacity as a dial: transparency "reduces the level of algorithmacy required." The scope conditions at line 155 now read as a switch: the construct "applies where a computational intermediary is opaque, adaptive, and mutually binding, and it ceases to operate under static administrative rules." The pre-cut Scope section had the bridge — "Arrangements fall along a continuum according to how strictly the three properties govern the coordination episode" — and the cut removed it. Without it, line 153's "reforms that eliminate the three properties dissolve coordinative co-optation" and Proposition 3 describe two geometries, one categorical and one graded.

Fix: restore the continuum sentence at line 155 (twenty words), and let Proposition 3's gloss point back to it. While there, correct line 195: P1 and P2 follow from the capacity/standing decoupling and the uneven-distribution property; P3 follows from the derivation (less opacity, less *interpreting* to do) and the Traeger result the robotics block already ties to it at line 83. "Three propositions follow from this tension" is true of two.

### Finding 6 — The coordination-form argument counts its forms three ways and treats Selznick's mechanism as a form without saying why (severity: low-moderate)

**Lines 17, 23, 31, 33; Appendix A line 431.** Line 17: "three canonical coordination forms." Line 31: "Four classical forms" (adding classical co-optation). Line 33: "This fifth coordination form," attributed in the same sentence to Stark and Vanden Broeck, who count platforms fourth after command, contract, and collaborate. Appendix A: "five archetypal coordination forms." The counts reconcile once the reader realizes the author's fourth is Selznick, but the body never says why Selznick's *co-optation* — in *TVA and the Grass Roots* an adaptive mechanism by which an organization absorbs a threat, not a way of coordinating interdependent work — earns standing as a coordination form beside Powell's three. The paper needs Selznick for two things only: the gloss that keeps a reader's intuition for "co-optation" from misfiring (line 23) and the seat-versus-queue-position contrast (line 33). Neither requires promoting classical co-optation to a form.

Fix: at line 31, say that classical co-optation enters the comparison as the compound term's etymological anchor and nearest contrast, and that Paper 1 — not Stark and Vanden Broeck — is the source of the five-form count. Then use one count throughout.

### Finding 7 — "Participant standing" oscillates between a gap in the theory and a fact about the form (severity: low-moderate)

**Lines 33, 35, 153; Appendix A line 437.** Line 33: coordinative co-optation "does not address *participant standing* at all" — a form does not address anything; a theory does. Table A1: "Participant standing remains theoretically unformalized" — a claim about the literature. Line 33 again: "the enrolled participant receives a queue position" — a claim that standing is *absent*. Line 35 resolves it the right way ("regardless of what standing an arrangement assigns") and line 153 builds the boundary on that resolution. It matters because capacity/standing is the paper's most important scope decision: if standing is substantively absent on platforms, that is a Paper 1 claim to state as inherited; if merely untheorized, "queue position" is rhetoric doing a theory's work.

Fix: pick the "untheorized" reading (it is the one line 35 and the construct section actually use) and rewrite the first sentence of line 33 to say the *scholarship* has not paired the mechanism with a standing. Keep the queue-position line as the image; it is good.

### Finding 8 — "Coordination" is never defined in the body (severity: low; an opportunity)

**Lines 17–35; Appendix A line 431.** Okhuysen and Bechky's three integrating conditions — predictability, common understanding, accountability — appear only in Appendix A, so the body never says what coordination requires and a reader cannot check whether the three operations deliver coordination or only survival. True pre-cut too. The opportunity: the operations map onto the three conditions almost one to one — *keeping track* restores predictability under a moving rule, *interpreting* and *specifying intent* rebuild common understanding across a channel that withholds grounding, and accountability is what evaluative attribution (RQ4) is about. That mapping ties the construct to the OT coordination literature the venue expects, and it gives a *theoretical* prior on the fourth-operation question the paper currently leaves to the data alone. One paragraph in the Tripartite Model section.

### Finding 9 — Line 17 over-generalizes from one paper (severity: low; created by the cut)

**Line 17.** "The research that has followed workers into them (Zhou et al., 2025) leaves the human counterpart outside the analytical frame." Pre-cut this was a claim about one program; compression made it a claim about all platform fieldwork, and the Rahman hearing (line 127) concedes Rahman interviewed eighteen clients. Fix: "the construct-building research (Zhou et al., 2025)."


### Smaller points

- **Line 175** says the survey "establishes" and the critical incident technique "shows" — present tense for work that fields in spring 2027 (line 221). "Is designed to establish / to show." Content, not style: a reader takes it as a result.
- **Line 159**, the Rahman relation: "discriminant validity requires low-to-moderate latent correlations" with Reactivity — a construct with no instrument, and the pre-cut rationale (adaptation-versus-withdrawal under discipline versus communicative orientation and tracking) is gone. Restore the rationale and say how reactivity will be operationalized in Study 2.
- **Title, lines 9, 21, 181**: "competency," "interpretive sensibility," and "capacity" all name the construct. Line 181 settles the category as competency; one clause saying "sensibility" names what the competency is like from the inside would stop a Suddaby reader asking which term is the definition.

## Part 3 — What is genuinely strong (do not "fix")

**The derivation is on the page, and it is good.** Line 165 does in three sentences what the prior round asked for — one property, one deficit, one operation, with bindingness generating *specifying intent* because a bound message is evidence before a verdict — and line 171 states the claim's width exactly ("it is the whole of what the phrase claims"). Keep both.

**The site paragraph discloses instead of asserting.** Line 215 states the reviewer-side binding mechanism, the algorithmic assignment of reviewers, and the rule-level retuning, and separates rule drift from codebase drift with the coding protocol's exclusion attached. It answers last round's most damaging finding completely.

**The pilot incident is told as a rival outcome.** Lines 173, 221, and 524 agree: machine-only routing, peer never addressed, Table B1's disconfirming pattern, protocol changed. Three passages, one story, honest N.

**The Simmel frame earns its place twice.** Line 41's *tertius* positions warrant treating the intermediary's status as form-fixing; line 151's arbitrator-without-conferral is the sharpest single sentence about what a platform is that I have read in this literature.

**The inheritance discipline survived the cut.** Every hearing still closes on what algorithmacy takes and rejects, and Study 3's covariate list (line 566) cashes those inheritances out as design commitments — a literature review whose conclusions are operationalized in the methods.

**The Paradox section remains a model of normative restraint.** Line 193's turn of Elish against the construct itself is the self-directed hazard analysis referees ask for and rarely get.
