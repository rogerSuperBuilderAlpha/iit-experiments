## Verdict

I would keep reading, and this time I did not have to push. The student is now the first thing on the page, "co-optation" is glossed on page two, and the three operations come with examples bolted on. Those three changes fix the three things my predecessor ranked highest, and the paper is easier to enter than it was.

It is harder to stay in. The cut took eight thousand words out of the middle, and in about a dozen places it removed the sentence that made the next sentence make sense: a gloss, a restating clause, a second example, the antecedent of a "that omission." None is fatal alone. Together they change the reading from "dense but continuous" to "dense, with potholes." I list them below with word estimates; the total to restore is under 450 words.

The paper's claim in my words: **when a black-box algorithm sits between you and the person you are working with, grading you both by rules neither of you can see and that quietly change, getting the job done takes a distinct, learnable skill — reading the machine's verdict for what the hidden person meant, writing for the machine and the person at once, and noticing when the rule has moved. This paper names that skill, shows that no existing theory has a slot for it, and says how it will be measured.** I could write that sentence from the Introduction alone this round. Last round it took the whole paper.

### Abstract and Introduction

Abstract (line 9).** *Understood:* a new skill for a new arrangement; three parts; a study to test it. *Lost:* "the triad imposes" arrives before any triad has been mentioned, and "behavioral micro-foundation" still means nothing to me. *Fix:* one clause — "the three-way arrangement, or triad, that results" — and "an account of what the individual inside it must be able to do" in place of the jargon.

**2. The opening scene (line 15). Works.** The paragraph my predecessor asked to have moved to the front is there. One student, one visible thing and one hidden thing, and a closing sentence that states the three operations in plain words before the paper has named them. I understood the paper here and never had to revise that understanding.

**3. The fragments paragraph (line 19). Works, mostly.** Bucher's users guessing what the feed wants, Litt's imagined audience, Manky's drivers reading a score to judge whether a stranger is safe — each is a person doing a thing. *Lost:* "a single determination binding both sides of a jointly executed task" is the whole argument in eleven words; I got it on the second read. *Fix:* one plain restatement — "in none of them is the other person also being graded, and in none are the two building something together."

**4. Lineage and definition (line 21). Works.** Literacy, numeracy, oracy, algorithmacy: the family carries the coinage, and the three defined terms are defined where they arrive.

**5. "Paper 1" (line 23).** A bare proper noun; line 27 explains it four sentences later. Pre-existing, not a cut cost. *Fix:* "The first paper of this dissertation names…"

**6. The co-optation gloss (line 23). Works.** "It does not hire you, buy from you, or befriend you — it enrolls you, on terms it sets, scores, and can revoke." Selznick's seat sits right beside it, so my wrong intuition gets corrected on the spot.

**7. Why naming matters (line 25). Works, and shrank.** The invisible-inequality stake is in the Introduction now. What the cut removed was the sentence that made it physical: in the pre-cut text this paragraph also said that reading a black-box metric is how a Lima driver decides whether a stranger is safe to pick up. The sharpest stake in the paper, gone. *Fix:* restore it (about 25 words).

**10. Table 1 (lines 47–57). Works.** The Structural Vacancy column is my map through the section.

**11. Human–machine communication, first block (lines 65–71). Followable, then insider.** Sundar and Nass — same message, four cover stories about who sent it, four different reactions — I understood, and I saw why it grounds *interpreting*. Then line 69: "The relocation is one seat vacated and another taken within a two-position model" has no antecedent for "the relocation." The pre-cut text had a paragraph on Guzman (2018) explaining it — the discipline assumed technology was a channel between two humans; a machine built to do the talking takes one of the two seats. The paragraph went; the noun stayed. Line 71's "structurational critique" is undefined. *Fix:* one sentence restoring the relocation (about 40 words); "Gibbs et al.'s (2021) objection from inside the field" for "structurational critique."

**12. AI-mediated communication (lines 75–79). Works.** Smart replies, Airbnb hosts trusted less when suspected of machine writing, the "moral crumple zone" glossed on arrival, and "An editor is not a judge." The hearing a lay reader can follow end to end.

**13. Machines among groups (line 83). Works.** A robot that admits error makes the humans talk more equally; the link to Proposition 3 is stated.

**14. Asymmetric interpretation (lines 87–89). Fast.** Suchman: the person brings everything, the machine holds a thin trace, so the person does the repair. Then "the same thin trace is also what prices the counterpart" — "prices" stopped me. The pre-cut text followed it with a restatement I could hold: "Suchman's user interprets a machine holding a thin trace of her; the participant here interprets a verdict that fuses that trace with a second person's judgment she cannot see." The cut kept the compressed clause and dropped the plain one. *Fix:* restore it (about 30 words). The trajectory paragraph at line 89 works.

**15. Computer-mediated communication competence (lines 93–99). Works.** Spitzberg's technology is a phone line between two people who judge each other; here the line is also the judge. Clark and Brennan's grounding is the best-explained borrowed theory in the paper: a message is done when both parties have evidence it landed, and here no evidence ever comes back. "Grounding cannot close." *Lost, mildly:* line 99 uses *specifying intent* and *interpreting* as technical terms seventy lines before their definitions; the Introduction's plain version carries it, just.

**16. Social information processing (lines 103–107). Works — the strongest hearing for an outsider.** Walther's people learn to pack feeling into timing and word choice; that works because the person you write to is the person who answers, and in the triad the one who answers (the gate) is not the one who matters (the peer). "The student at the gate can write her pull-request description a hundred ways, and what the failed verdict tells her is that this one did not clear." *Lost:* "pull-request description" is never glossed; the Introduction said "submits her code for review." *Fix:* "the note a programmer attaches when submitting code for review" at first use.

**17. AI literacy (lines 111–113). Works.** Seventeen competencies, two mention humans, both offstage. The contrast at 113 — their list was assembled by scoping criteria, this one derived from what the arrangement withholds — I followed and wanted cashed out.

**18. Algorithmic competency (lines 117–121). Works, one detail lost.** Items 8, 11, and 6 are the most concrete discrimination in the section: the courier appeals to the platform and the customer who rated him is never in the room. The pre-cut version had audio recordings and timestamped logs; the cut kept "compiles evidence." "The socialization property stated below" (line 121) points at a property line 185 calls "acquired through participation." *Fix:* match the label.

**19. Reactivity (lines 125–129). Works.** Five properties of the cage, two kinds of reactivity, clients who never knew what their ratings did, and the paragraph I most respect: if dependence and shock history explain the variance, "algorithmacy explains nothing a labor-process account has not already explained better." A paper that names its strongest rival that plainly earns trust.

**20. Gig literacies (lines 133–135). Works.** P39 takes her clients off-platform; escape, not mastery. *Lost, mildly:* the heading says "gig literacies," the text formalizes "platform literacy," and line 159 says "the four subdimensions." Three names for one construct.

**21. What these boundaries share (lines 139–147). Works, with one cold reference.** The folk-theories rival (line 141) is the paper's most honest paragraph: yes a triad, yes opacity; the difference is that the algorithm binds only the presenter and nothing is built together. "Three dimensions dispose of it" opens as a reference to a list that arrives two sentences later — recoverable. Line 143 is the problem: "Online dispute resolution theorized the infrastructure as an active 'fourth party.'" What is online dispute resolution? Fourth after whom? The cut plan reduced this to a sentence because "the Introduction already names it," and the Introduction's mention of Katsh and Rifkin was cut in the same pass. The term now appears cold. *Fix:* "Scholars of online dispute resolution — systems that settle eBay-style disputes without a human judge — had already called the software a 'fourth party' beside the two disputants and the mediator (Katsh & Rifkin, 2001)." About 35 words. The four conditions (line 145) read as a referee's checklist; I trusted Table 1 instead. The Lima drivers (line 147) remain the paper's best evidence and still get two sentences at the tail of a section a tired reader is skimming.

### The construct

**22. Opener (line 151). Still inside baseball.** The Suddaby sentence is shorter and still a sentence about the rules for writing the sentences that follow. The Simmel arbitrator after it is good: the platform decides between the two like an arbitrator, but nobody appointed it and nobody can fire it.

**23. "That omission" (line 153). Dangling.** The paragraph opens "That omission is where the construct's own boundary starts," and the previous paragraph ends on "the only withdrawal on offer is exit." Nothing was called an omission. The pre-cut text had the antecedent — "Conferral is exactly what the arrangement omits, and that omission…" — and the cut removed it (12 words). The same paragraph invokes "the methodological individualism Gibbs et al. (2021) criticize"; the hearing at line 71 no longer says Gibbs criticized any such thing. *Fix:* "the charge that individual-level constructs ignore the institutions shaping them (Gibbs et al., 2021)."

**24. Scope (line 155). Works.** Dispatch queues and hidden-rubric peer review at the core; freelance platforms at the edge because you can negotiate or leave.

**25. Semantic relations (line 159).** For specialists. The one part I could hold: someone who believes machines are objective may report confidence without any skill, and the paper wants to be sure it measures the skill and not the belief. Say that plainer; the rest can stay technical.

**26. The tripartite model (lines 163–175). The heart, and it mostly works.** Daft and Lengel's split — you have plenty of data, what you lack is a way to settle which reading is right — landed at once. Then line 163 ends: "Dennis, Fuller, and Valacich (2008) name what goes missing as *convergence*." Full stop; never glossed, never used again. The pre-cut sentence continued: "— the process of reaching shared meaning about information both parties already hold — which their theory shows requires the rapid mutual feedback the triad withholds." The cut kept the term and lost the meaning. The three-deficits-to-three-operations derivation (line 165) and the bullets (167–169) are the best-improved passage in the manuscript: each operation ends on a moment from the student's life. **Are the three operations distinct to me?** Yes — decode, encode, notice — and the bullets are why. Line 171's "That mapping is what the phrase 'derived from the structure of the triad' means in this paper" defends a quoted phrase that appears nowhere else (pre-existing). *Fix:* "That mapping is the whole of what I mean by derivation." RQ4 is invoked at 171, fifty lines before the research questions.

**27. Why each operation needs the triad (line 173). Compressed hard.** The pilot participant who fed her explanation to an external language model and never addressed the peer is here, plainly told — good. Around it the cut removed the two sentences that set up *specifying intent* (in ordinary online writing, encoding means fitting a channel; here the channel is also the judge) and the two that set up *keeping track* (where you can read the rubric, a rule change announces itself; under opacity it does not, so "the primary evidence of algorithmic change surfaces in what the counterpart does next"). That last clause was the plainest statement of the paper's most interesting idea — you learn the algorithm changed by noticing the people changed — and my predecessor singled it out. "Dual-audience encoding" is also used here as a name before it has been introduced as one.

**28. The process paragraph (line 175).** "Neither reflective indicators of a single latent trait nor formative components of a static index" — the pre-cut version glossed both halves (a person can be good at reading and bad at writing; what you notice updates what you read next). Now a psychometrics sentence sits in a plain paragraph. *Fix:* restore the glosses (about 30 words) or move the sentence to Appendix C.

**29. Competency, not skill or capability (line 181). Works.** Skill you can teach; capability the firm owns; competency the person holds. "Molar impression" is undefined here and at line 225. *Fix:* "overall impression."

**30. Acquisition and distribution (line 185). Works.** Learned by doing; spread unevenly; four rivals named and sent to Study 3. "SNAIL scale" is a bare acronym.

**31. Theoretical implications (line 189). The compression hurt.** The first three sentences I followed: labor-process scholars study control, this studies execution, so you know where to intervene. Then one sentence carries what was 330 words: "Online dispute resolution names what the arrangement withholds — transparency, accountability, and informed participation (Wing, 2016) — and algorithmic regimes forfeit each while directing contestation to the platform's own apparatus rather than to the counterpart." Why does dispute resolution get to name what a platform owes? Who is Wing? What does "directing contestation to the platform's own apparatus" look like? The pre-cut text told me: Curchod's sellers who can appeal only to the same rating system that scored them. *Fix:* restore that example (about 25 words) and gloss Wing's principles as "a code of ethics the dispute-resolution field wrote for itself."

**32. The paradox (line 193). Made sense, moves too fast.** *Understood:* being good at this helps you and changes nothing; worse, a theory that explains outcomes by worker skill risks blaming the worker for the system's opacity — the crumple-zone move from line 77 turned on the construct itself. A real worry, stated against itself. *Lost:* the pre-cut sentence between the paradox and the fairness claim — algorithmacy is "a capacity to operate within a regime rather than an endorsement of its fairness" — is gone, so "capacity and fairness vary independently" lands bare. The Cameron sentence jumps from her workers to "an enrolled population's collective competency" with the middle step removed. *Fix:* restore both (about 45 words).

**33. The propositions (lines 197–201). Made sense.** Helps you, fixes nothing; sorts identical workers and the platform calls it merit; more transparency, less skill needed. P3's gloss, "an alternative governance pathway to compensatory competency," is two abstractions where the pre-cut had one plain sentence.

### Empirical strategy (lines 213–221)

**34. Did it tell me what will happen?** Yes, once I assembled it: about fifty students in a sixteen-week course in spring 2027 write real software, submit it to the gate from the Introduction, get interviewed by an AI inside their code editor about specific incidents, and take three surveys at weeks 1, 4, and 16; the ethics board has approved it; two pilot interviews exist and one is unread. A straight answer, and "none of the formal corpus has been collected or coded" is the sentence I trust most. *Lost:* "Hult" (line 221) is a bare word — the appendix says Hult International Business School, the body never does. "AI-administered critical incident interview protocol, embedded in the development environment" I decoded into "an interviewing chatbot inside the code editor"; say that. "Core action research project" versus "formal research project" (line 213) I trusted rather than followed. Line 215's three gate properties work, but the last sentence on exit rights no longer says why they matter. The pre-cut did: "A student's option to drop a course is not the analogue of unilateral account termination." Restore it (18 words).

### Conclusion (lines 225–227)

**35. Does it earn the Introduction?** Half. The Introduction promised a name for a skill, a reason the name matters, and three moves. The Conclusion delivers boundaries and modesty and closes on the aggregation question. It never returns to the student, the inequality, or Lima. "Advisory decision aids, dyadic software tools, and unmediated peer collaboration" (line 225) are three exclusions stated as categories; the pre-cut had the physician, the caseworker, and the dating app behind them, and those told me what the boundary meant. "Molecular" and "molar" recur undefined. *Fix:* a closing paragraph that puts the student back at the gate and says what naming her skill lets someone do for her (about 60 words), plus one clause of examples for the exclusions.

## What the cut cost the reader

The plan aimed at depth, not scaffolding, and mostly hit its target. Where it missed, the pattern repeats: a paragraph compressed to its claim, and the clause that made the claim intelligible went with the depth. Place, what the pre-cut text had, and the cost to restore:

1. **Line 25** — the Lima driver deciding whether a stranger is safe to pick up. ~25 words.
2. **Line 69** — Guzman's relocation: channel assumption broken, one seat taken. ~40 words.
3. **Line 87** — the plain restatement of Suchman's asymmetry in the triad. ~30 words.
4. **Line 143** — what online dispute resolution is and why the software is a "fourth party"; the Introduction's mention was cut simultaneously. ~35 words.
5. **Line 153** — "Conferral is exactly what the arrangement omits, and…" 12 words.
6. **Lines 71/153** — the charge Gibbs et al. actually made. ~20 words.
7. **Line 163** — the *convergence* gloss. ~25 words.
8. **Line 173** — *specifying intent*'s setup (encoding fits a channel; here the channel is the judge) and its closer ("a demand that participants can and do decline"). ~45 words.
9. **Line 173** — *keeping track*'s setup, ending "the primary evidence of algorithmic change surfaces in what the counterpart does next." ~40 words.
10. **Line 175** — the two glosses on the psychometric-form sentence, or move it to Appendix C. ~30 words.
11. **Line 189** — Curchod's sellers with no appeal outside the rating system; a gloss of Wing's principles. ~35 words.

Total about 430 words, four percent of the body. Two cuts I do not want back: the Introduction's "frameworks fail in different ways" preview (Table 1 does it) and the literacy-limits paragraph.
