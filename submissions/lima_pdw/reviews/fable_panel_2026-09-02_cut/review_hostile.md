## Verdict

**Major revisions, one kill-shot.** The demarcation from Spitzberg and Walther rests on a counterpart who is *unseen* and *returns nothing the actor can read* (lines 9, 97, 105, 167, 173). The opening scene says the opposite — "Her classmates read it, comment on it, and vote, all in public where she can watch" (line 15) — and Appendix B confirms that at Hult "qualitative peer reviews and upvotes remained publicly inspectable" (line 479). The peer replies, in prose, in public; what is opaque is the aggregation of her reply into a verdict. That is a transparent peer dyad plus an opaque grader — exactly the "ordinary hybrid of pedagogical hierarchy, peer review, and automated grading" line 215 says three gate properties refuse. They refuse it on the machine side. Nothing refuses it on the counterpart side, because the counterpart is not hidden.

The vacancy claim survives at its narrowed width, as a claim about *constructs* — and it now has a ninth candidate the paper never heard, from the literature the site actually lives in.

## Part 1 — Kill-shots and attacks

### 1. The counterpart at the site is visible and replies; the demarcation from CMC competence and SIP assumes she is neither. — **Severity: fatal if unanswered**

Lines that carry the construct's novelty: the abstract's "*interpreting* the intermediary's rules and the unseen counterpart's intentions from the same composite feedback" (line 9); the Clark and Brennan argument that "the counterpart returns no acknowledgment the actor can read, no next turn addressed to her, and no route to repair ... Grounding cannot close" (line 97); the Walther argument that "which of her two readers the sentence lost, and where, is not in the signal" (line 105); "she has one signal from which to infer two minds" (line 173); "the unseen human counterpart" in the operation's own definition (line 167).

Lines that describe the site: "Her classmates read it, comment on it, and vote, all in public where she can watch" (line 15); "Qualitative peer reviews and upvotes remained publicly inspectable while the underlying aggregate weighting remained concealed" (line 479).

Those two sets cannot both be true of the same arrangement. At Hult the student has two signals, not one: a public prose review addressed to her pull request, and an unexplained verdict. She can read what the peer meant and reply in the thread. Grounding *can* close on meaning; what cannot close is grounding on *consequence* — how much the peer's judgment counted. That is a real deficit, but it is Rahman's (opaque weighting of a client's rating), not a communicative one, and Spitzberg's competence plus Rahman's reactivity cover it between them without a third construct. A referee puts it this way: the paper derives its operations for a triad in which the intermediary stands *between* the parties and severs their channel; the site has the intermediary standing *above* two parties who keep a direct channel. The "hard case" framing (line 490: "the opacity does not come from hidden source code; it comes from concealed aggregation weights") concedes this without noticing.

**What survives / the fix.** Two honest routes. (a) Restate the deficit as opacity of *consequence*: the peer's meaning is legible, her weight is not, and *specifying intent* becomes encoding for a reader you can see and a judge you cannot — a smaller claim that needs "unseen" nowhere. Then argue in one paragraph why this is not CMC competence summed with reactivity; the answer has to be that the *same act* is read by both, which the pilot's machine-only routing illustrates. (b) Keep the strong claim and admit the site does not instantiate it, which pushes the program to Study 4's dispatch settings. Route (a) is the paper's; route (b) is fatal to Paper 3. Either way, "unseen" at lines 9, 121, 167 and 168 must go or become true.

### 2. The three-property derivation derives only the machine-facing half of each operation; the counterpart-facing half rides on a property the paper never names as one. — **Severity: severe**

Line 165 now performs the mapping the last round demanded: "Opacity withholds the rule, so the actor must reconstruct it. Bindingness turns every message into evidence before a verdict, so the actor must encode for judge and counterpart at once. Adaptivity unsettles whatever the actor has learned." Read each clause for what it licenses. Opacity licenses reconstructing *the rule*, not the counterpart's intentions. Bindingness licenses encoding *for the judge*; "and counterpart at once" is smuggled in, since a binding verdict on a solo task imposes no dual audience. Adaptivity licenses maintaining a reconstruction of the rule, again. Three properties, three deficits, three *dyadic* operations. Every counterpart clause — the paper's whole novelty — comes from elsewhere: the absent grounding loop (line 171) and "interdependent joint work" (lines 9, 19, 141, 225). Neither is a scope condition (line 155 lists three: opaque, adaptive, mutually binding) and neither is in the derivation. The abstract's vacancy claim requires joint work; the construct's scope conditions do not. "Three properties, three deficits, three operations" is a numerological match, not a derivation.

**Fix.** Add a fourth scope condition — the parties execute interdependent work and the intermediary is their only channel of consequence — and redo the mapping: opacity plus channel closure → two minds from one signal; bindingness plus joint work → dual-audience encoding; adaptivity plus channel closure → tracking a rule through the counterpart's behavior. Four properties, three operations, and "each operation requires the triad" (line 173) becomes derivable rather than asserted. Stated as closure of *consequence*, not visibility, this also answers Attack 1.

### 3. A ninth candidate the paper never hears, from the field the site lives in: algorithmically weighted peer assessment and student feedback literacy. — **Severity: severe**

The paper draws its eight candidates from "organization theory, communication studies, and human-computer interaction" (line 27). The site is a peer-assessment system in a credit-bearing course (lines 466, 488), and peer assessment with algorithmically weighted, reliability-tuned aggregation is a twenty-year literature the paper does not open. From memory, to be verified before citing back — I have no network: Calibrated Peer Review (Russell and colleagues, UCLA, early 2000s) already binds the *reviewer* through a competency index that feeds her own grade; Piech, Huang, Chen, Do, Ng and Koller (2013, "Tuned models of peer assessment in MOOCs") estimate per-grader reliability and reweight grades by it — concealed, adaptive weights on submitter and grader, at scale, in the wild; Kulkarni et al. (2013, *ACM TOCHI*) on peer assessment in massive classes; and Carless and Boud (2018) name *student feedback literacy*, an individual capacity for giving and using peer feedback.

Two consequences. A discussant who teaches will name this literature in the first five minutes, and the "no construct" claim will look like a claim about three fields rather than about the literature. And — this helps the author — the site is then an instance of a known class with a design history, not a one-off the researcher built, which answers the purpose-built charge (Attack 4) better than anything on the page. What survives: none of these sources, as I recall them, models a capacity to coordinate *through* the aggregator with the peer as an interdependent party; feedback literacy is dyadic and the reliability models are system-side. The vacancy holds. The hearing is missing.

### 4. Who retunes the rule? The no-intervention pledge and the adaptivity claim cannot both hold as written; and the form's anchoring sanction is absent at the site. — **Severity: severe**

Line 213: "I will execute no further interventions within the focal cohorts." Line 215: "the rule itself moves: weights and thresholds are retuned over the term." Retuned by whom? The passive hides the agent, and only one person can be behind it: the course designer, who is the researcher. If he retunes during the Paper 3 term, that is an intervention, the pledge is false, and adaptivity is an experimenter manipulation confounded with the outcome it is meant to elicit. If he does not, the rule does not move and last round's Finding 5 returns. The escape is a retune mechanism that is automated or pre-committed — thresholds recomputed from the cohort distribution each sprint, weights on a schedule fixed before week one — and the paper must name it. Line 215's "no identifiable office administers a legible rule (Weber, 1978)" is also false on the natural reading: there is an office, the instructor's, and it administers an *illegible* rule. That is arbitrary authority in Weber's sense, not algorithmic enrollment.

The second half. The abstract defines the form by an algorithm that "can revoke their accounts" (line 9; again at 23). A course grades and, at worst, fails; it does not deactivate, and line 215 concedes exit and escalation rights are undocumented. So the site instantiates the *construct's* three properties — all design parameters the author set ("gate refinements varied information visibility," line 468) — and lacks the *form's* anchoring sanction: a purpose-built analog of the construct dressed as an instance of the form. The honest sentence: "The site instantiates opacity, adaptivity and mutual bindingness by design; it does not instantiate unilateral termination, which Study 4's settings test." Attack 3's literature gives a better sentence still — the site instantiates a class of peer-assessment systems that exist independently of him.

### 5. Simmel's arbitrator, stripped of conferral, is not an arbitrator; and the cut removed the reading that was right. — **Severity: serious**

Line 41 lists Simmel's three third positions — mediator, *tertius gaudens*, the third who manufactures opposition — and says which one the third occupies "fixes the arrangement's form." Line 151 then picks a fourth, the arbitrator, and removes its defining condition in the same sentence: "Simmel's arbitrator holds an authority the two parties conferred on him and can withdraw; the platform holds an authority neither party conferred." Simmel's mediator/arbitrator distinction turns *entirely* on prior submission by both parties (the source card confirms the Wolff reading). Take the submission away and what remains is not an arbitrator with a difference; it is the third who profits from the two never connecting — Simmel's *tertius gaudens*, Burt's (1992) broker holding a structural hole open, Johnson's (2004) broker managing information flow so neither party routes around him. The pre-cut text had that paragraph (archive line 222); the plan called it "not load-bearing." It was: it is the paper's only account of *why* the intermediary withholds — the disconnection is the asset — and the correct Simmelian label. The arbitrator label borrows the legitimacy of a submitted-to judge and then denies the submission. A sociologist in the room will say so.

**Fix.** Eighty words: the platform is *tertius* by structure and arbitrator by self-description; Burt explains why the first is stable, Johnson what the third must do to hold it; the far side of that position is where the construct lives.

### 6. The propositions cross the capacity/standing line the paper draws twenty lines earlier. — **Severity: serious**

Line 153, after the last round: "The boundary this article draws runs between capacity and standing — not between micro and macro, a distinction the propositions below deliberately cross." Good. Now the propositions. P1 (line 197): algorithmacy leaves "the arrangement's *procedural justice* unchanged" — standing. P2 (line 199): variance "introduces a secondary axis of *inequality*" — a standing outcome. P3 (line 201): transparency is "a malleable *institutional lever*" — governance. All three predicate standing outcomes of a construct whose stated boundary is that it makes no such predication. Either the construct's *content* stops at capacity and its *implications* reach standing — fine, and it should be said — or the boundary sentence is decoration.

Two of the three are also weak as propositions. P3 is analytically true given the scope conditions: if opacity is a scope condition, removing it removes the need for the capacity by definition, and a site where transparency failed to reduce required algorithmacy would simply be reclassified as not transparent. "The level of algorithmacy *required*" (line 201; Study 4, line 575) is not a measurable quantity; the panel measures algorithmacy *held*. P1's second clause — gains concentrate in private navigation, not in disclosures won or appeals granted — is testable; its first clause is near-tautological, since a capacity defined as private navigation will not, by construction, change procedure.

**Fix.** Rewrite line 153 to say the construct's content is bounded at capacity and its propositions relate capacity to standing without adjudicating it. Rewrite P3 as an empirical claim about *held* algorithmacy's predictive power under disclosure variation, and drop "required."

### 7. The Paradox section names the crumple-zone hazard and then performs it in the same paragraph. — **Severity: serious**

Line 193: "a framing that reads variance in outcomes off variance in worker capacity is itself, structurally, a crumple-zone move." Three sentences later, same paragraph: "Cohorts exposed to identical gates but differing in mean algorithmacy should therefore differ in attrition, contestation volume, and coordination failures." That is variance in outcomes read off variance in worker capacity, with the arrangement held fixed and the workers as explanans. Study 3 says it outright — the design "quantifies the operational and human costs an organization incurs when this competency is scarce" (line 568) — and the Introduction's remedy sentence, "cannot be measured, taught, or compensated for" (line 25), locates the intervention in the individual. Elish's mechanism is that responsibility attaches to whoever stands visibly nearest the failure; a construct that makes the worker's capacity the visible, measured, teachable variable puts her there. "Holding capacity and fairness apart" is a conceptual distinction, and Elish's point is that conceptual distinctions do not stop the attribution.

**What survives / the fix.** The paradox is real. The self-application fails because the hazard demands a design commitment and the paper offers a distinction. Commit: every capacity-side estimate in Studies 3 and 4 is reported beside an arrangement-side estimate (disclosure level, appeal availability, retune frequency) and the variance is partitioned between them, so the construct never appears in a results table alone. Said in the Paradox section, that turns P3 from decoration into the mechanism that keeps P2 from being a crumple zone.

### 8. DeVito (2021) is the rival for *keeping track*, for acquisition-through-participation, and for uneven distribution, and it is uncited. — **Severity: serious**

The folk-theories rebuttal at line 141 disposes of Bucher (2017), DeVito et al. (2018) and Litt (2012). It does not mention DeVito's (2021) "Adaptive folk theorization as a path to algorithmic literacy on changing platforms" (*PACM HCI* 5, CSCW2, article 339), which the project's own card calls "the most dangerous single source in this sub-cluster." DeVito adds a *change-detection* element — theorization that adapts when the platform changes — which is the third operation named and evidenced; a four-level *Theorization Complexity Level* classification of users of equal standing on one platform, which is line 185's uneven distribution observed; acquisition through participation rather than instruction, line 185 again; and a definition derived from what changing opaque platforms deny the user, uncomfortably close to line 113's derivation-versus-inventory contrast. The three rebuttal dimensions still hold — no counterpart, no binding, no joint work — but the paper rebuts the 2017/2018 versions and leaves the 2021 one, with change detection and stratified fluency, for the discussant. DeVito also designs explicitly for *teaching* the literacy, the strongest published case against line 185's claim that instruction cannot cultivate it.

**Fix.** One paragraph at line 141 or 185: concede the pairing of interpreting and adapting as prior art; hold the counterpart and the binding.

### 9. Two empirical claims that a reader with the sources open will reverse. — **Severity: moderate**

(a) Line 121: "the primary antecedents of algorithmic competency in their structural model are social — informal peer support moderated by collectivist orientation." The project's full-text read of Zhou et al. records Table 6, Step 2: peer social support b = .23, cognitive job crafting b = .41. The larger antecedent is an individual cognitive mechanism, so defined by the authors; the paper's only cited support for the socialization property points, on the numbers, the other way, and the card does not confirm any collectivist-orientation moderation. Report both coefficients or drop the sentence.

(b) Line 159 calls the predicted null with *Embracing* "the sharpest single test in the network." Zhou et al. already found *Embracing* at r = .04, non-significant, with generic digital competence, and it is the weakest-linked dimension internally (.22–.25). A null with an attitude scale is what every skill-like measure produces there; it discriminates algorithmacy from nothing in particular. The sharp test in that paragraph is the machine-heuristic partialling. Give it the label.

### 10. Every control the hearings promise is cashed in a study with no sample and no site. — **Severity: moderate**

Walther's cognitive slack (line 107), Rahman's dependence and shock history (line 129), Nass and Moon's mindlessness and Sundar and Kim's machine heuristic (line 185) all "enter Study 3 as baseline controls." Study 3, line 566: "Sample and setting remain to be specified." The next sentence — naming the covariates makes the inheritance claims "design commitments rather than concessions" — is exactly a concession when the design has no participants. Move the covariates into the Paper 3 panel (short scales exist for each) or stop presenting them as answered.

### 11. The psychometric form is now self-contradictory, and the cut did it. — **Severity: moderate**

Line 175: the operations are "neither reflective indicators of a single latent trait nor formative components of a static index." Appendix C, line 556: Study 2 "powers a confirmatory factor analysis," a reflective model. The pre-cut text (archive line 286) reconciled the two — the panel treats the dimensions as separable for discriminant testing while the incident protocol traces the loop. The cut kept both halves of the contradiction and removed the reconciliation. Restore two sentences.

### 12. Smaller content defects. — **Severity: minor**

Manky (line 147) now sits outside the paper's own domain: screening a passenger for danger is not "interdependent joint work" (line 19), and the narrowing made the prior flag bite. Line 17 counts "three canonical coordination forms," line 31 "four classical forms." Line 25's "attributes the outcome to merit" is an uncited claim about platform ideology. ## Part 2 — Defenses the cut removed

I read the pre-cut archive against the plan. The folk-theories rebuttal **survived** essentially intact (line 141), as did Gibbs et al. and the methodological-individualism answer (lines 71, 153) and the Sundar and Nass grounding of *interpreting* (line 65). Those were the right things to keep. The following were removed, and each now exposes a claim.

1. **Johnson (2004) / Burt (1992) brokerage** (archive line 222, ~180 words; gone). Defended against: "why does the intermediary withhold?" and "which Simmelian third is this?" Exposure: Attack 5 — the arbitrator label stands alone and is wrong; and the paper no longer has any structural account of withholding, so opacity reads as a design accident rather than as the asset. **Restore at ~90 words**, in the construct opening (line 151), replacing "arbitrator" or qualifying it.

2. **Katsh and Rabinovich-Einy (2017) and the contestation-channel argument** (archive line 313, ~330 words; reduced to clauses at lines 143 and 189). Defended against two questions at once: "does this arrangement exist outside your classroom?" — eBay's resolution center is determinations at volume, binding on parties who never meet, without reasons, by a neutral the platform owns — and "why is the counterpart structurally unreachable?" — because no principle in the fourth-party literature names a right to route a dispute anywhere but the platform. Exposure: Attack 4's purpose-built charge and Attack 1's channel-closure premise both lost their best external witness. **Restore ~120 words** at line 189.

3. **The SAGE Handbook inventory** (archive line 86; one sentence at line 69). Defended against "you infer a field's unit of analysis from one agenda article": a handbook's taxonomy, not Guzman and Lewis, establishes where the field sets its unit, and multi-party settings *do* appear there as a machine among people rather than between them. Exposure: the HMC vacancy claim now rests on one article plus an assertion about sixty-five chapters. **Restore ~50 words.**

4. **The Conclusion's boundary cases**, above all matchmaking (archive lines 392–396; gone). Dating is the cleanest real-world case of mutual bindingness under opacity, and the paper's answer — it fails *joint execution* — was the only place the second half of the two-part claim was shown excluding anything on its own. Exposure: line 225 asserts "mutual bindingness *and* joint execution" with no case for either half. **Restore ~60 words**, the dating case alone; drop the preprint-based clinical bullet for good.

5. **The 2026 adjacent-scholarship sentence** (archive line 388; gone). Hong, Cheng and Liu (2026), "Working the algorithm: Contextual skills of on-demand gig workers," is a nine-indicator competence construct on Zhou's population, published this year, in *Big Data & Society*. Its absence now means a discussant can name a same-year rival the paper has not seen. The project has read it in full; the discrimination (peers appear, the customer never does) is sharp and cheap. **Restore one sentence, ~40 words**, at line 225.

6. **The literacy-metaphor-limits paragraph** (archive line 272; gone). Its last sentence — "*keeping track* has no equivalent in traditional literacy frameworks, because conventional media hold still" — was the single best justification of the third operation and the answer to "why not call this algorithmic literacy?" With DeVito (2021) uncited (Attack 8), the third operation now has neither its justification nor its rival on the page. **Restore that one sentence, ~20 words**, at line 169 or 173.

7. **The psychometric-form reconciliation** (archive line 286; gone). See Attack 11. **Restore ~40 words** at line 175.

8. **Lower-priority losses, optional.** UNESCO (2024) "human accountability in human–AI decision loops" (archive line 212) answered "isn't this a call for an accountability framework?" — algorithmacy begins after the erosion UNESCO tries to prevent. Healy and Pekarek (2025) (archive line 220) showed the triad "can be carved two ways," consumer-as-third or algorithm-as-third, which was the cleanest statement of what the paper chose. Bunz and Montez (2015) (archive line 292) — no CMC-competence instrument survived a platform change — supported line 181 and is also a threat to Study 2's one-gate instrument; its removal hides the threat rather than answering it. Iyamu/Nutbeam's communicative literacy (archive line 210) was the concrete near-miss that made the coupling argument at line 139 visible.

Total restoration for items 1–7: roughly 420 words, putting the body near 10,650. That is the right trade.

## Part 3 — What survives even a hostile read

The eight hearings remain real readings, and the cut sharpened rather than thinned them: the item-level Zhou analysis, the page-cited Rahman client interviews, Sutherland's P39, and the Walther loop-closing argument — the best paragraph in the paper, and the one Attack 1 obliges the author to re-aim rather than delete. The abstract states the vacancy at the narrowed width and the Introduction earns it against Bucher, DeVito 2018 and Litt in one paragraph. The pilot incident is narrated honestly in three places (lines 173, 221, 524) and is stronger for it. The derivation at line 165, incomplete as Attack 2 shows, is a derivation and not an announcement of one. The machine-heuristic threat (lines 67, 159, 185) is a discriminant worry most construct papers never think to raise. And the two-part claim at line 225 — mutual bindingness *and* joint execution — is the right claim; the paper's remaining job is to make its scope conditions, its derivation and its site agree with it.
